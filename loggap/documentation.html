Worklog Coverage for Jira — a read-only Jira dashboard gadget

What it answers

Of the work items your team completed in the last N days, how many have no time logged against them — and whose items are they?

It does not tell you how many hours were logged. It tells you whether time tracking is happening at all on the work you finished.

Setting it up

  1. Add the gadget to a Jira dashboard.
  2. Open its settings and enter a JQL filter for the scope you care about, for example project = ABC or project in (ABC, DEF) AND issuetype != Sub-task.
  3. Set the window — how many days back to look for completed items. 30 is a reasonable start.
  4. Press Preview counts before saving. It shows how many completed items match and how many already have no logged time. If the total is 0, your filter or window is wrong.

What the app adds to your filter

Your JQL is only the scope. The app always appends two conditions:

  • statusCategory = Done — only completed work is counted
  • resolved >= -Nd — completed within the window

So you cannot use this gadget to ask about work in progress. That is deliberate: work that is not finished yet has no reason to have time logged against it, and counting it would make the number meaningless.

Reading the numbers

  • Coverage % — completed items with any logged time, divided by all completed items in scope. The denominator is always shown next to it. With fewer than 10 items the percentage is a hint, not a measurement, and the gadget says so.
  • The bars — per assignee. Green is items with logged time, amber is items without. Note that timespent is the whole item's total across everyone who logged against it, so "Ann's coverage" means "of the items assigned to Ann, how many have any time logged" — not "how much time Ann logged". A tester logging onto someone else's item counts there.
  • The gap list — the most recently completed items with no logged time, newest first, because those are the ones still worth chasing. The full JQL for the list is shown under it; paste it into Jira's issue search, or wire it into your own automation.

Limits worth knowing

  • At most 2,000 completed items are scanned, newest first. When that limit is hit the gadget says so: older completions were not counted. Because recent items are more likely to have time logged, a truncated result tends to look better than reality.
  • Results are cached for up to 1 hour.
  • Sub-tasks are included or excluded according to your JQL — add issuetype != Sub-task if you do not want them.
  • The gadget reads only what the signed-in user can see, so two people may see different numbers on the same dashboard.
