Common questions

The macro says my formula could not be parsed

The message names the line and column, shows that line of the source and draws a marker under the position where typesetting stopped. The most common causes are an unclosed brace (\frac{a}{b), a command that does not exist (usually a typo), or an environment that was opened with \begin{…} and never closed.

I wrote an align block and it says it needs a line of its own

Environments such as \begin{align} only work in display mode. Wrap the whole block in $$…$$ or \[…\] rather than $…$.

Can I put a formula in the middle of a sentence?

Yes. One macro can hold normal text and formulas together — write the sentence and put the maths in $…$ where it belongs, for example The area is $\pi r^2$ for a circle. Text stays text, formulas are typeset.

Does this macro send my formula anywhere?

No. See the Security and Privacy pages.

Which LaTeX commands are supported?

Whatever the bundled typesetting library supports, with one deliberate exception: commands that would produce links or pull in external content are disabled — \href, \url, \includegraphics and \htmlClass are shown as text rather than acted on. Commands that come from extra LaTeX packages which are not bundled are not available, and the error message will say the command is unknown.

Are there limits?

Up to 20,000 characters and 500 lines per macro. Formulas are typeset on the page, not exported — there is no download in this version.
