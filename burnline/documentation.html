Burnline: Burnup & Burndown with Forecast

1. What the app does

Burnline draws a dashboard gadget with two lines and the gap between them:

  • scope — how much work is on the books
  • delivered — how much of it is finished
  • the gap between the two lines is what is left to do

It then takes the rate at which the team has actually been finishing work and projects when the remainder will be done. That projection is the part that is hard to get from a screenshot of a board.

2. Requirements

Jira Cloud. No other app, no admin action beyond installing it, and no external service.

3. Add the gadget to a dashboard

Open a dashboard, choose Add gadget, and search for Burnline. Then open the gadget's settings and fill in the filter.

4. Settings

SettingMeaning
JQL filterWhich work items are in scope. Everything not yet done is always included, however long it has been idle.
Done statusEntering this status counts as delivered. Picked from your project's status list.
History windowHow many weeks of history to chart and to measure throughput over. Default 12. Maximum 104.
Target dateOptional. Draws a vertical line on that day, so the chart can show whether the projection lands before or after it.

5. How the chart is computed

For any day d in the window:

  • scope(d) counts the items that existed on day d
  • delivered(d) counts the items finished by day d
  • remaining(d) = scope(d) − delivered(d)

An item counts as delivered at the moment it last entered the Done status you chose. If it was reopened and finished again, the later moment is used.

6. The forecast, percentile lines, and sample size

Throughput is measured in whole 7-day periods ending today, so a half-finished week is never counted and cannot drag the projection backwards. Every week is kept, including weeks in which nothing was finished — a week with zero deliveries is data, not a missing value.

The projection is weeks remaining = items remaining ÷ throughput, evaluated at four percentiles of those weekly counts. This is exact rather than approximate: dividing by a throughput value is a monotone transformation, and percentiles are equivariant under monotone transformations, so dividing by the 50th-percentile throughput gives the 50th-percentile completion time — not an estimate of it.

A percentile line is drawn only when the window has enough whole weeks to support it:

LineWhole weeks of history required

Below the threshold the line is omitted and the reason is printed under the chart, instead of being drawn from too few observations. A projection beyond two years is also omitted.

7. What the chart does not claim

Read this before treating a forecast date as a commitment.

  • The scope line is today's set of items traced backwards by creation date. Items that finished before the history window and were not touched since are not in it, so the early part of the scope line is lower than the true historical scope.
  • The projection assumes throughput continues like the history window. If the team changes, it changes. It is a projection, not a schedule.
  • All counts are work items and all time is calendar days, not working days.
  • Items in a done-category status that never passed through your chosen Done status are excluded and reported — they are neither counted as delivered nor as remaining.
  • If a filter matches more items than the app scans, the chart says so. A truncated scan understates what is remaining and makes the projection too early.

8. Languages

English and Simplified Chinese. Statuses are stored by ID rather than by name, so the gadget keeps working regardless of the language Jira is displayed in — and it is unaffected by two different statuses that happen to share a name.

9. Data and privacy

The app reads only issue keys, creation dates, statuses and status transition timestamps, as the viewing user, and caches the computed numbers for one hour. It makes no external network requests at all. Full detail in the privacy policy.
