Privacy Policy

Issue Templates for Jira

Last updated: 5 October 2026

This app is published on the Atlassian Marketplace by the Marketplace partner flowscope (referred to below as "we"). This page describes exactly what the app does with data. It is written to match the app's source code, not to describe a general policy.

This app writes. Unlike a reporting gadget, it creates an issue in your Jira site — that is the whole point of it. It creates only the one issue you reviewed and confirmed, in a project you picked, when you press the create button. It never edits, transitions, deletes or bulk-creates anything, and nothing at all is created before you press that button.

1. What the app reads

All requests are made through the official Jira REST API as the user who is using the app, using the read:jira-work scope. The app can only ever see what the person using it is already allowed to see; it never uses an administrator identity and it cannot gain access to anything on its own.

  • Your projects. It asks Jira for the projects in which you are allowed to create issues, and shows you that list. It does not request the projects you cannot file work in.
  • The issue types, components and priorities of the project you select. This is what lets the app offer only the templates and values that project actually accepts.

That is the entire read. The app does not read issue descriptions, comments, attachments, worklogs, issue history, or user profiles. It does not read any other issue in your site, and it does not read any other project.

2. What the app writes

When — and only when — you press the create button, the app creates one issue containing:

FieldWhere the value comes from
ProjectThe project you selected
Issue typeThe template's issue type, or the one you changed it to
SummaryThe template's summary, as you edited it
DescriptionThe template's text, as you edited it, converted to Jira's document format
Labels, components, priorityThe template's defaults and your edits — only values the project itself offers

The issue is then an ordinary Jira issue. It is visible to whoever the project's permission scheme allows, exactly like an issue you created by hand, and it is deleted, archived or moved by the same means as any other issue. We keep no copy of it.

3. What the app stores

Nothing. The app keeps no database, no cache and no per-user record. It uses no app storage at all: every action reads what it needs from Jira at that moment, and the result lives only in the open page in your browser.

There is no state to expire, no cache to clear, and nothing left behind after you close the tab. Uninstalling the app therefore has nothing to delete beyond the listing of the app itself.

4. Logs

On a successful creation the app writes a single line to the Forge runtime log, which is hosted and retained by Atlassian like any other Forge app's log. That line records the issue key that was created, the project key, the Atlassian account ID of the person who created it, which template was used, and how many fields had to be adjusted. It is used to diagnose failed or unexpected creations.

It does not record the summary, the description, or any other content of the issue — only the identifiers needed to find it. No log is sent to us or to anyone else; it stays in Atlassian's log for the app.

5. What the app does not do

  • No external servers. The app makes no network request to any host other than Atlassian's own Jira REST API. There is no egress, no webhook, no callback URL and no third-party integration of any kind.
  • No analytics or telemetry. No usage tracking, no error-reporting service, no cookies, no fingerprinting.
  • No advertising. No ad networks, no trackers, no profiling.
  • No selling or sharing. We do not sell, rent or share any data with anyone. There is nowhere for it to be shared to.
  • No AI or machine-learning processing. No data is sent to any model or inference service. The templates are fixed text stored in the app; they are not generated.
  • No data outside Atlassian. All processing happens inside Atlassian's Forge runtime, in the region your Jira instance is pinned to. The only thing written outside Forge is the Jira issue itself, inside your own Jira site.
  • No deletion or modification. The app creates issues. It never edits, transitions or deletes an issue, and it never creates more than the one you confirmed.

6. Sub-processors

None. Atlassian provides the platform (Forge runtime and logs) under your own agreement with Atlassian. Beyond Atlassian, no other company processes any data on our behalf.

7. Retention and deletion

  • The app stores nothing, so there is nothing of ours to expire.
  • Issues it created are ordinary Jira issues and follow your own retention rules. Deleting one is a normal Jira delete and requires no involvement from us.
  • The log line described in section 4 is retained by Atlassian under the same terms as other Forge app logs.

8. Your rights

Because the app holds no data of its own, a data-subject request is normally satisfied by deleting the issue (if you want the issue gone) or by uninstalling the app (if you want the log purge to stop accumulating). If you need something specific, contact your Jira administrator first — they control the installation — and you may also contact us at the address below.
